## Who this is for

- **Professionals** trying to work out why they've stalled, when they've clearly got the capability.
- **Leaders and L&D teams** who've run the training and bought the tools and still can't explain why adoption is uneven.
- **Anyone building an adoption programme** who wants a diagnosis that goes deeper than "give them another course."

If you've ever thought *I know enough — I'm just not sure I'm the one who does this*, this model is about you specifically.

## The six stages

Sorted by internal experience, not tools deployed:

1. **Nomad** — defensive. *"Why now, and why for someone like me?"*
2. **Observer** — curious but stuck. *"Is it safe to start?"*
3. **Dabbler** — excited but inconsistent. *"Is the investment worth what I'll get back?"*
4. **Integrator** — quietly confident. *"What could I build with this, for other people?"*
5. **Architect** — restless but hesitant. *"Do I actually believe I'm the kind of person who does this?"*
6. **Innovator** — building outward. *"What's broken in my field that nobody fixes?"*

The gap that matters most isn't Stage 1 to Stage 6. It's **Stage 4 to Stage 5** — and it has almost nothing to do with skill.

## Start here: locate yourself

The [stage locator](framework/stage-locator.md) is a two-minute self-assessment. Pick the statement you recognise before you've finished reasoning about it — that's usually your stage, and it tells you what's actually holding you (which is rarely what you'd guess).

## Where this came from

This isn't a whiteboard model. It's drawn from delivering AI training and adoption work with real organisations — an SMB marketing agency, a national ministry team, and professionals across sectors — where the same pattern kept showing up: the people who stalled weren't short on skill. They were waiting for permission.

The stages describe that pattern. The Architect stage, in particular, is the one standard maturity models don't have a name for, and it's where I've watched the most capable people sit longest.
