# Stage Locator

A short self-assessment. It won't take five minutes. Read the six statements below and pick the one that lands first — the one you recognise before you've finished reasoning about it. That's usually your stage.

If two feel close, you're probably moving between them. Pick the one that describes most of your week.

---

**1. "I've decided AI isn't for me — or at least not yet, and not for someone in my role."**
→ You're a **Nomad**. The next move isn't more evidence that AI works. It's one reason that connects to something you already care about. Read [the Nomad stage](the-six-stages.md#stage-1--nomad).

**2. "I'm interested and I've been watching, but I haven't really started."**
→ You're an **Observer**. What you need isn't more information — it's one low-stakes attempt where it doesn't matter if it's messy. Read [the Observer stage](the-six-stages.md#stage-2--observer).

**3. "I've tried it, it's surprised me once or twice, but I don't use it consistently."**
→ You're a **Dabbler**. The unlock is one or two tasks where AI works *every* time, so the wins stop being luck. Read [the Dabbler stage](the-six-stages.md#stage-3--dabbler).

**4. "AI is a genuine part of how I work now. I save real time and I know what it's good at."**
→ You're an **Integrator** — further than most people get. The question waiting for you: what could you build with this *for other people*, not just for your own output? Read [the Integrator stage](the-six-stages.md#stage-4--integrator).

**5. "I can see something I'd like to build, and I understand the problem well — but I haven't committed to being someone who builds it."**
→ You're an **Architect**. This is the longest-held stage, and the barrier isn't skill. Read [the Architect stage](the-six-stages.md#stage-5--architect) — it's the one most models skip.

**6. "I'm already building things other people use — and I keep noticing what's broken that nobody's fixing."**
→ You're an **Innovator**. The work now is choosing which problem is worth your attention. Read [the Innovator stage](the-six-stages.md#stage-6--innovator).

---

## What to do with your result

The stage isn't a grade. It's a diagnosis of what's actually holding you — which is rarely what you'd assume.

- If you landed in **1–3**, your next step is about lowering the cost of trying.
- If you landed in **4**, your next step is a question, not a course: *whose problem do you keep noticing?*
- If you landed in **5**, the honest work is identity, not technique. Proximity to a peer one stage ahead moves this faster than any tutorial.
- If you landed in **6**, you already know. Pick the problem.

> **The point of the model:** the thing stopping you is usually not the thing you'd name first. Most capable people assume they need more skill. Far more often, they need permission.
