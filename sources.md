# Sources and Verification Notes

Last reviewed: 2026-07-24.

This repository is a practitioner-built model. It is intended as a reflective framework for AI adoption work, not as a validated psychometric instrument, academic maturity model, or substitute for organisational assessment.

## Status of the Model

The six stages were developed from AI Tutorium delivery experience, including AI literacy and adoption work with small businesses, professionals, and ministry teams. The model's distinctive claim is that AI adoption often stalls because of identity, confidence, perceived permission, and the meaning people attach to "being someone who uses or builds with AI", not only because of skills or tool access.

That claim is offered as a practitioner synthesis. It should be treated as a useful lens to test in context, not as settled research.

## External Concepts That Informed the Framing

- Technology adoption and diffusion research, including the idea that adoption is social and uneven rather than purely technical.
- Change-management practice, especially the distinction between awareness, desire, knowledge, ability, reinforcement, and local sponsorship.
- Self-efficacy research: people's belief in their capability affects whether they attempt, persist with, and generalise new behaviours.
- Adult learning and professional identity: experienced adults often need relevance, safety, autonomy, and social proof before changing established work habits.
- AI literacy guidance: effective AI adoption requires task judgment, critical evaluation, and responsible use, not only tool fluency.

## Verification Boundaries

- The stage locator is a reflective self-assessment. It has not been statistically validated.
- Stage labels are interpretive categories, not fixed traits.
- People may move between stages by task, context, role, or organisational setting.
- Claims about what "moves" each stage are practitioner recommendations, not guaranteed interventions.
- Field reports and case studies should document context, audience, setting, what changed, and what did not change.
